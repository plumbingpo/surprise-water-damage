Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Once a year, renew the caulk at sinks, showers and tubs to keep water from entering the wall.
  • Keep leftover flooring, tile and paint details so future repairs can be matched.
  • Do not paint or patch over a water stain until the source is fixed.
  • Curing time for paint, floor adhesive and caulk should pass before the room sees water or hard use.
  • Laundry rooms, bathrooms and kitchens should be rebuilt with moisture resistant wallboard and proper backer board.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Tiles sound hollow, have come loose or show cracked grout since water got beneath them.
  • Baseboards and door casings are warped or no longer sit flat against the wall.
  • Laminate or vinyl plank edges are swollen, curled or separating at the seams.
  • You see brown stains, peeling or bubbles in paint along the path the water took.
  • The subfloor gives or feels spongy underfoot where water once stood.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • A door frame or slab swelled, so the door rubs or fails to close.
