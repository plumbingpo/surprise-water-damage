Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Surprise

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our Surprise team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Insulation that is wet no longer insulates, and the added weight can pull the ceiling drywall down.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Our Process

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Our crew logs the readings and lets you know as soon as the area is ready for a replacement heater.

Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
  • Where the heater sits, with attic and interior closet leaks costing more than garage leaks.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.
  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.
