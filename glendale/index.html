Common Water Damage Issues in Glendale

Water usually escapes at the same few vulnerable spots in a home. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Glendale owners.

Water heaters: Over the years, mineral deposits settle in a tank and corrode the metal underneath. Look for moisture at the base, rust streaks on the jacket or a small pool that keeps coming back. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. Heaters in a hallway closet or attic can ruin flooring and ceilings well beyond the utility area.

Roofs: One strong storm is enough to peel back shingles and push water beneath the roof covering. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. On Glendale roof leak calls, our crew removes saturated insulation, sets drying equipment and can cover the opening until a roofer arrives.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. Outdoor spigots, sprinkler feeds and pipes in crawl spaces or along outside walls are hit first. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. We remove any standing water in Glendale homes, dry the walls and slab, and explain what likely caused it.

How Long Drying Takes in a Glendale Home

Touch is a poor test for whether a wall or floor has dried. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. Our Glendale technicians carry probe meters for wood and wallboard, scanning meters that read through finishes, and infrared cameras that pick up damp areas as cooler spots.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Drywall and carpet give up moisture fast, but studs, subflooring, hardwood and concrete are slow. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. Most jobs finish in 3 to 5 days, and if yours will take longer you will hear it from us at the start.

Stopping early is the costly mistake. Materials closed up while still damp can grow mold, swell, or come loose months later, long after the job seemed finished. So for every Glendale job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.
