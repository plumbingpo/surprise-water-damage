Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Surprise

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Growth Behind the Paint

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.
