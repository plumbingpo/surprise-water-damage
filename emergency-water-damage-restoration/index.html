Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Damage Restoration?

Mopping the puddle in front of you is rarely the real challenge during an emergency. The difficulty lies in tracking where the remaining water travelled. Water runs under baseboards, into wall cavities and beneath flooring within minutes. With a thermal imaging camera and moisture meters we locate that hidden water and treat it the same night, before it turns up weeks later as an odor or a stain.

Truck-mounted extractors, commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers matched to the room come with the crew, and no household keeps those around. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. When it comes time to file a claim, that documentation helps. You get one team that handles the emergency and stays with the job until the home is dry.

Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Shut off the water at the main whenever you leave home for more than a few days.
  • Run a check on the sump pump ahead of wet weather, and think about adding battery backup.
  • Save an emergency restoration company's number in your contacts ahead of time.
  • Find your main water shutoff today and make sure everyone in the house can close it.
  • Place leak sensors near the water heater, washing machine, dishwasher and under sinks.
  • Insulate pipes in unheated spaces so they do not freeze and split in cold weather.

When to Call for Emergency Water Damage Restoration in Surprise

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
  • Carpet, drywall or hardwood has been soaked for more than a few hours.
  • What is on the floor came up from the sewer, or it is discolored and smells foul.
  • Floor-level power strips, plugged-in appliances or the breaker panel are now wet.
  • An outlet, a light fixture or the ceiling above has water dripping out of it.
  • A leak upstairs is sending water down the walls of the level beneath it.
  • Part of your ceiling bows downward because water is pooling on top of it.
