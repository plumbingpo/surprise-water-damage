Kitchen Water Damage Help in Surprise

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. We help Surprise homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
