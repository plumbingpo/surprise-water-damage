Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Surprise, AZ

Home / Blog / Preventing Sewage Backups in Surprise, AZ

A sewage backup is one of the most unpleasant emergencies a homeowner can face. Raw sewage entering your living spaces creates immediate health hazards, causes extensive property damage, and requires professional cleanup that can cost thousands of dollars. Learn the reasons these backups happen, act on a few preventive measures, and you cut the odds considerably. This guide from Surprise Restoration covers the common causes of sewage backups in Surprise, AZ and practical steps you can take to protect your property.

Why Sewage Backups Happen

Sewage backups occur when wastewater cannot flow from your property to the municipal sewer system or septic tank as intended. The most common causes include: clogged sewer lines from grease, hair, and debris buildup; tree root intrusion into sewer pipes; aging or collapsed sewer pipes; municipal sewer system overloads during heavy rainfall; combined sewer overflow events; and improper items flushed down toilets including wipes, feminine products, and excessive paper.

Tree Root Intrusion

Roots from nearby trees are a leading reason sewer lines in Surprise get blocked. Because roots grow toward water, a pipe that always carries it draws them in. Entry happens at connection points, cracks, and joints, and over time the growth packs the pipe, creating the blockages behind many backups. Older clay tile sewer pipes are particularly vulnerable to root intrusion. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

How to Prevent a Sewage Backup

Protecting your Surprise home from sewage backups involves several preventive measures: install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent municipal system backflows. Never pour grease or cooking oil down drains. Only flush toilet paper, never wipes, feminine products, or other items. Have the sewer line inspected and cleaned on a regular schedule. A drain that is running slow needs prompt attention, or it may block completely. Find out where your sewer cleanout is so it can be reached in an emergency. It may also be worth looking at sewer line coverage or a backup endorsement for your homeowner's insurance.

What to Do If Sewage Backs Up

Cleaning up a sewage backup on your own is something you should never try. Serious illness can come from the dangerous pathogens in sewage, among them parasites, bacteria, and viruses.
